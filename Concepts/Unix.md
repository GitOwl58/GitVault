# Unix

An operating system originally developed by Ken Thompson and Dennis Ritchie at AT&T Bell Labs, first released in 1970. Introduced the design principles that most modern OSes still follow: everything is a file, small composable command-line tools, a hierarchical filesystem, and multi-user/multi-process support. Written in C, which made it portable across hardware — a major reason it spread widely through universities and industry.

Unix itself splintered into many commercial variants over time (Solaris, AIX, HP-UX, BSD). [[Linux]] is not Unix code, but a Unix-like clone: Richard Stallman's GNU project aimed to build a free Unix-like OS in 1983 and produced most of the userland tools plus the GPL license, but lacked a working kernel until Linus Torvalds started the Linux kernel in 1991. Combined, "GNU/Linux" recreated the Unix philosophy as a free, open-source system.
